At my last company, we ran several websites on Markdown content: blogs, event listings, and landing pages. Markdown is great until you need non-developers to publish updates. Then we developers become the bottleneck.

We were looking for CMS tools that plug into our existing sites, instead of forcing our code into their framework.

We first tried <Brand icon="decap">Decap CMS</Brand>. It's free and open-source, but we had to maintain a `config.yml` that grew with every content type, and still fetch and validate the Markdown ourselves, with no type safety.

Then we gave <Brand icon="tina">TinaCMS</Brand> a try. It handled fetching and typing, but made us query our own files through GraphQL, and its git workflow confused developers and editors alike.

Two tools, same result: more time spent on the CMS than on the content. **I just wanted it to be simpler.**

I still believed in Markdown as a content source, and the timing made the case even stronger. AI agents now work alongside you in the codebase, and they're only as useful as the content they can read. A hosted CMS keeps that content on someone else's servers, behind APIs that can rate limit you, go down, or charge for access.

<Underline>The best way to give agents your content is to keep it local</Underline>, in your repo, right next to your code. Owning your content layer has never mattered more.

So I built <Brand icon="qino">qino</Brand>, and it comes with:

1. **Type-safe content.** Define your content with Zod schemas. Types hold from end to end.
2. **No config file to maintain.** qino generates it from your schemas and settings.
3. **No framework lock-in.** qino plugs into the site you already have.
4. **Any content shape.** Collections, standalone pages, or full documentation sites.
5. **A cloud editor, soon.** Non-developers [edit content](/cloud) without touching code.
6. **A CLI for agents.** `qino check` validates every edit against your schemas.

You can use `@qino/cms` as a [standalone](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@qino/cms) content pipeline for your files, or pair it with the cloud UI for a full CMS experience once it ships.

**Give qino a try!** [Read the docs](/docs) or let your agent [set it up](https://www.skills.sh/charlymartin/qino/set-up-qino-cms).
