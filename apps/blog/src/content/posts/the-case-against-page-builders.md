Page builders feel empowering for the first month. Then you realize you've signed a lease on someone else's tooling.

The pattern is familiar:

1. Marketing wants to ship a landing page without a developer.
2. You install a page builder.
3. Six months later, *every* page is in the page builder.
4. You can't move off it without rebuilding everything.

The trade-off was real — fewer developer cycles in exchange for a layout you can't easily migrate. The catch is that the migration cost compounds the longer you stay.

The flat-file alternative is to give marketing a small, opinionated set of *components* — a hero, a feature grid, a CTA — that they compose in Markdown via shortcodes or MDX. Constrained but composable. Hard to misuse, easy to migrate.

The right amount of flexibility for a marketing site is *not infinite*. Page builders forget that.
