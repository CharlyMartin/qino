Frameworks come and go on a roughly five-year cycle. The Markdown you wrote in 2014 still renders today — the framework you wrote *around* it probably doesn't.

This is the quiet superpower of plain text: it's not coupled to any runtime. You can move from Jekyll to Hugo to Astro to a homegrown Next.js app without touching the content itself.

The lesson generalizes:

1. Keep content boring.
2. Keep tooling replaceable.
3. Don't let the second one infect the first.

The frontmatter convention helps here too — a few lines of YAML at the top of a file, parseable by anything.
