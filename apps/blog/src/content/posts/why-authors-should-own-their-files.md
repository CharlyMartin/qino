The contract between an author and a publication should be simple: *the author owns their words*. The publication owns the venue.

In a SaaS CMS, that contract is murky. The author writes into a textarea, hits "Publish", and the words live in someone else's database. Leaving the publication means leaving the words behind, or asking nicely for an export.

A flat-file CMS makes the contract literal. Each post is a file. The author can `git clone`, take a copy, and walk away with everything they wrote. The file works in any editor, on any platform, in any decade.

This is mostly a *philosophical* argument, not a technical one. But the technology either supports the philosophy or it doesn't. Files do.
